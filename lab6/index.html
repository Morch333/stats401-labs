Design note

Why the layouts look different

Both treemaps use rectangle area for GDP because it shows each country as part of the world total. I used green for an increase, gold for no change, and red for a decrease; the same palette appears in both views so the layout is the only structural difference. Continent and area labels sit above their country cells, while the tooltip gives the full path, exact GDP, and status when a label does not fit. The squarified layout produces compact rectangles, which makes area comparison and country labels easier. Its branches are less obvious at first glance, so I added heavy continent outlines and lighter area boundaries. Slice–dice alternates the direction of subdivision at each level. The resulting bands make continents and areas easier to follow, but small economies become thin strips and are harder to compare precisely. For this dataset, I would use the squarified view for comparing GDP and the slice–dice view for tracing the hierarchy.
