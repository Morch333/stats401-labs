Force-directed diagram of 50 transit stations. Station color represents district, size represents daily passengers, shape represents station type, route color represents service type, and line width represents travel time.

Why I used these encodings

District color makes neighboring areas easy to compare, while station area gives busy stops more visual weight. I used different shapes for local, transfer, and terminal stations because those are categories, not amounts. Route color separates the three services, and thicker lines mark longer travel times. Hovering removes unrelated detail; dragging is useful when two labels overlap.

Symmetric adjacency matrix for the same 50-station network. Rows and columns are grouped by district. Filled cells are direct routes, color represents route type, and opacity represents travel time.

Why I ordered the matrix this way

Both axes list the same stations, so every filled cell is a direct route. I grouped the labels by district and colored the margin bands to make district-to-district blocks visible. Cell color keeps route type, while opacity keeps travel time. The matrix trades the network’s overall shape for exact pair lookup and makes isolated stations show up as empty rows and columns.

03

What I found

Well · Node-link

Which stations are central?

There is no single hub: the highest degree is only three. Hovering shows Stations 7, 18, 21, 29, and 33 among the well-connected shortcut stations near the core.

Well · Matrix

Which districts connect strongly?

The densest off-diagonal block is East–South with 11 routes. Central–North and East–West follow with nine each; the district ordering makes those blocks countable.

Partly · Node-link

Where are transfer and terminal stations?

Diamonds and squares occur across every district and along the main chain. Most terminals are not endpoints, so station type does not closely match topological position.

Well · Node-link

Which stations are busiest?

The largest symbols belong to Stations 43–50. Station 50 is largest at 9,850 passengers but has only one route; several other high-volume stations are isolated.

Well · Both views

Where are the longest routes?

The thickest link and darkest Express cells join Stations 7 and 31 at 16 minutes. Stations 18–34 and 29–50 follow at 15 and 14 minutes.

Partly · Matrix

Are route types concentrated?

The three cell colors are spread across the matrix rather than forming a clear block. Counts are nearly even—17 Express, 17 Shuttle, and 16 Metro routes.
