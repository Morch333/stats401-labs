Design reflection

Why voice works here—and where it does not

I extended the coordinated GDP choropleth and cartogram from Lab 9 with voice commands. A user can say “highlight China,” “show Europe,” or “reset” to select a country, filter both views by continent, or restore the full display. This supports quick geographic lookup while the user keeps attention on the maps instead of searching through menus. Voice is especially useful when a small country is difficult to click or when the cartogram has moved circles away from familiar borders. The page repeats the recognized phrase and reports the resulting action, so a mistaken command is visible rather than silent. In testing, country names with multiple common forms—such as United States/U.S. and South Korea/Korea—needed aliases. Background noise, accents, browser support, and microphone permission can still cause errors. For that reason, speech begins only after the user presses Start listening, and the same selection, filtering, and reset actions remain available through dropdowns, mouse clicks, keyboard focus, and buttons.
